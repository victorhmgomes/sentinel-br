VASP fraud intelligence · Brazil · public data only

Bank attacks this year that left a signal on crypto exchanges.

Part of the money diverted in bank fraud moves through crypto venues — and because these are public markets, that stretch of the path can be observed from outside. Sentinel BR tracks price and volume across 16 venues every 15 minutes and flags when the market pattern resembles a cash-out in progress. It is one more signal for prevention teams, not a replacement for internal controls. In the Sinqia / HSBC case, that signal appeared seven days before disclosure.

The attack happens inside the bank. One of its echoes shows up in a market anyone can read.

06

Method, and what we don't know

Calibration is re-run every night against live order books. Below, today's numbers and the honest limitations.

Calibration. Twenty live order-book baselines are collected every night at 03:00 UTC. False positives are measured leave-one-out; true positives by injecting six synthetic attack signatures and sweeping thresholds.

Limitations. Public data only — no VASP-side flows, no wallet attribution. Daily granularity carries a high base rate of "some alert somewhere"; intraday cross-venue signals are the discriminating ones. With public data, incidents under ~R$ 10M in quiet weeks can pass unnoticed — three did between June and August 2026; HourlyCorr is the bet to close that gap.
